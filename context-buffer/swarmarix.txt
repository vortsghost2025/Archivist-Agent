This one maps very directly to SwarmMind

The paper is “MAS-Algorithm: A Workflow for Solving Algorithmic Programming Problems with a Multi-Agent System”, submitted May 7, 2026. It proposes a structured multi-agent workflow for algorithmic programming problems, inspired by competitive programmers and algorithm engineers. The abstract says the workflow decomposes the end-to-end solving process into modular stages, enabling structured reasoning, tool integration, and coordination among agents.

That is basically the algorithmic-problem-solving version of what you keep doing in your lanes.

Core claim

The authors argue that instead of only improving the model itself, you can improve performance by wrapping models in a structured multi-agent process.

Their results claim:

MAS-Algorithm:
  benchmark: self-constructed benchmark
  average acceptance-rate gain: +6.48%
  fine-tuning comparison: +0.89%
  LiveCodeBench-Pro gain: +4.72%
  ablations/replacements:
    individual agents contribute up to +27.7%

Those are paper-reported results, not independently verified here.

Why it matters to you

The relevant part is not “competitive programming.” The relevant part is this pattern:

problem
→ decompose into stages
→ assign agents to stages
→ integrate tools
→ coordinate outputs
→ analyze errors
→ ablate agents to see which ones matter

That is exactly your missing measurement layer for SwarmMind.
